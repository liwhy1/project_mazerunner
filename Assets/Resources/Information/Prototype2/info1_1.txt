<b><i>The Surveyor</i></b>
Moldrovia's lost royal treasure lies across the valley. At a riverside campsite, three travellers
meet to seek it before a rival expedition arrives at hour eight. Your sources describe the same
world from different viewpoints. Study them alone before comparing maps.

<b>From your grandfather’s journal</b>
You inherited a survey journal and brought its sketches to the valley. A recent drawing shows
all three handles already fitted at the Chainlift. Only one engages the hauling mechanism; the
drawing does not identify it. Record their distinguishing shapes. Their numbers below identify
the pictures, not a sequence to pull.

<b>At the marsh entrance</b>
Yesterday, a scout stood at the fork where the road from Starting Point first enters the Marsh.
Facing towards the Marsh, the left path had the sign drawn below. The right sign was hidden
from the scout. Both paths continue through the Marsh to the Waystone; the scout did not
identify which is the raised causeway.

<b>A boundary survey</b>
A survey made yesterday places the whole Waystone-to-Bridge approach on the river side of
the wall. The wall curves from the village’s southern edge, along its eastern edge, to beside the
southwest bridgehead. The entrance stays clear. The survey locates road and wall, but does
not explain the curse.

<b>An old mountain survey</b>
Last year’s survey shows the Split Peak tunnel beside the Mountains crossing. Its East Gallery
is the only connection from the entrance chamber to the far exit; there is no other throughpassage.
It joined the Treasure road and made Mountains to Treasure take two points instead
of five. The survey gives no current condition. The traveller’s report concerns this same
entrance. East Gallery is inside the tunnel, not another landmark.
